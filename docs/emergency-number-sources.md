# Emergency contact corrections

The October 2026 review found non-emergency and specialized service numbers in
emergency fields. The corrected entries use these official references:

- [UK police](https://www.gov.uk/contact-police): 999 for emergencies.
- [Australian communications regulator](https://www.acma.gov.au/emergency-calls):
  000 for emergencies; 911 does not work in Australia.
- [German federal police](https://bundespolizei.de/kontakt/hinweis-geben): 110 for
  urgent police assistance; its service line is not the emergency number.
- [Norwegian police](https://www.politiet.no/english/contact-the-police): 112 for
  police emergencies; 02800 for non-emergency enquiries.
- [Danish police](https://politi.dk/en/contact-the-police/emergency-112) and
  [Swedish police](https://polisen.se/en/contacting-the-police/): 112 for emergencies.
- [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112):
  112 reaches emergency services throughout the EU. It replaces suspect local or
  non-emergency entries for Austria, Belgium, Czech Republic, Denmark, Greece,
  Italy, Lithuania, Netherlands, Portugal, Slovakia, Spain, and Sweden.

This is a targeted correction, not verification of every country in the legacy
dataset. Dial buttons accept only explicit telephone numbers and separate
alternatives into individual actions. Location-specific descriptions remain
visible but are not dialed automatically. Without a resolved country, the UI
does not recommend a universal emergency number.
